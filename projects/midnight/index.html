01 The brief

A store that feels like the objects it sells

Midnight sells painted candles and studio confections as art objects, in three collections: Light, Bite and Talk. The starting point was a night-sky page in Figma. The job was to turn it into a working store you can scroll, browse and check out from.

Role

Web design and front-end prototype

Scope

Home, store, product, cart, checkout, order confirmation and policy pages

02 Design & motion

A page that behaves like a night sky

The wordmark appears first, with a thread of light running through its letters. Then the clouds roll in and the moon rises. As you scroll, moons and clouds drift at different speeds, the three collection buttons appear after half a screen, and a statement lights up word by word. The intro plays once; returning visitors see the page straight away.

Cormorant Garamond for titles, Inter for text. Every “i” in a title carries a small candle flame.

03 Hover & interaction

Everything you point at answers back

Every control has a hover state, and none of them changes the size of what it sits on, so the page never jumps under the cursor. The same few moves repeat everywhere: things lift a few pixels, a light follows the pointer, and colour fills in from one side. Here is each one, recorded from the prototype.

Checkout speaks the same language: the payment tiles lift and brighten, the selected tile turns solid white, and the terms checkbox draws its tick. On the order page the moon follows the cursor, and a click sends a sweep of light across it.

04 Store & product

Three collections, one template

Filter chips with live counts switch between Midnight Light, Bite and Talk, and every “Explore more” on the home page opens the store already filtered. One product template serves every piece, and the cart is a drawer that remembers what you added.

05 Checkout & order

Checkout that fits a business or an individual

Buyers order as an individual or a business, enter shipping and billing addresses, choose Apple Pay, card or PayPal, add a voucher and accept the terms. After confirming, the order page draws a moon seal, shows the receipt, and the moon then follows the cursor. Prices are in Canadian dollars. The checkout is a prototype, so no payment is taken.

06 Every screen

The same night, down to a phone

The layout adapts from desktop to phone: a mobile menu, a checkout that puts the order summary first, and quieter backgrounds so text stays readable. The prototype is shared as a private preview. Its pages and images are encrypted and open with an access code.
